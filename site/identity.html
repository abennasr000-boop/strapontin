Brand identity · version 3.1 · 14 November 2026 · working document

Strapontin, a gallery you can book from

Version 3 follows the client's reference sheet from the mood board: sage paper, a visible hairline grid, wide serif capitals with one italic word, small extended sans labels. Texture, grain and instant-photo frames are gone. Posters are the only images and the only strong colour. Since 3.1 the home page is a mosaic of posters, and search and filters float in a light dock at the bottom of the screen.

01 · Goal

Playful discovery, clear facts

The main job, in the client's words, is playful discovery of films across venues, mostly on a phone. The identity therefore behaves like a gallery wall: quiet surroundings, the works do the talking, and every work carries its label (time, venue, price).

Rule: if an element is not a poster, a fact or an action, it probably should not be on the screen.

02 · Logo

Strapontin

The pen-drawn fold-down seat sits in a rounded square, as the monogram does on the reference sheet. The wordmark is set in wide serif capitals. The seat pad swings down on hover.

Status: the seat drawing is still a first sketch and needs a proper pass and a small-size test.

The paper moved from cream to the sage-beige of the reference sheet, and the ink from wine to a near-black wine. Raspberry and Golden Green survive only inside the venue illustrations and typographic poster tiles. This closes the Faded print / Dusk question: neither; the reference sheet decides.

Why so little colour: film posters are loud and all different. A neutral ground lets fifteen of them sit together without the page feeling heavy, which was the complaint about v2. Orangeade keeps a single meaning: book. Text contrast: ink on paper 12:1, muted text 5.6:1, Orangeade on paper 4.7:1.

  • Mosaic tile: a poster at one of three sizes, slightly uneven margins, no drawn lines. Title in capitals, director in italic and one line (next showtime, venue, price) lie on the image over a soft dark gradient.
  • Opened tile: the chosen film takes the centre at a size computed from the screen, with award, synopsis, tickets and bookmark; the others shrink around it.
  • Dock: a light, softly glowing pill floating at the bottom centre: search, when, area, version, gallery/list, surprise. An active filter turns ink. Same on phones, with shorter labels. First version, to be refined.
  • Ticket: the only outlined, coloured action. Stub lifts on hover.
  • Header: logo, three links, language, list. Nothing else.

06 · Rules

RuleBecause
The home page is the gallery. No hero text, no introduction.Client review of v2: the opening statement "took too much space for nothing".
Visual navigation first, deliberate filtering second.Client principle (F004): playfulness on one side, the film lover's own motivation on the other. The mosaic invites; the dock answers when you know what you want.
Lines are implied, not drawn.Client review of v3: the ruled grid felt rigid. Margins, text alignment and the edges of posters do the structuring.
Text on an image always sits on a gradient.Readability on any poster; keeps 4.5:1 contrast for the information line.
Every poster carries time, venue and price.Uncertainty avoidance (Hofstede brief) and the audit's core finding; discovery must not cost clarity.
Filters (when, where, version) and search stay on screen in the dock while scrolling.75% choose by proximity (CNC, as cited); students decide late; version matters to French and international viewers.
A list view is always one tap away.Some people come to compare times, not to browse. The audit asked for a booking tool, not a magazine.
No texture, grain, frames or shadows on content.Client review of v2: "too much information and textures".
One italic word per title; no other decoration.Keeps the reference sheet's gesture without the circled word the client rejected.
No urgency, no crowd signals; awards and programmers instead.Hofstede brief; unchanged since v1.

07 · Open points

  • Posters in this mockup are real film posters looked up on Wikipedia at view time. They are for internal review only, are not stored in the project, and their rights are not cleared. A real service needs distributor agreements. The published review page cannot load them and shows typographic tiles instead.
  • Critics' quotes were removed: with real films we will not invent quotes. Awards shown are factual.
  • The gallery-first home is now the stated product goal, but it is still untested with users (decisions D021, D024, D025).
  • The dock is a first version: light with a glow, at the client's request; its exact look and its phone behaviour will be refined.
  • The current test shows films only. Theatre is still in the product and returns when real artwork exists (D026).
  • Ideas logged for later: saved filters (I001) and a Letterboxd link (I002), both in the repository's ideas folder.
  • Poster files are low resolution (about 250 px wide); a real build needs proper artwork.
