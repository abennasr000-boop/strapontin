Brand identity · version 1 · November 2026 · working document

Strapontin, the seat there is always room for.

One place to find, compare and book a screening in the independent cinemas and pocket theatres of Paris. This sheet sets the identity and explains each choice by the audience it serves and the business goal it supports. The clickable prototype applies every rule below.

01Who it is for

The single success metric is engagement rate: people who come back, save films, follow venues and finish a booking. Each segment needs something different before it will do that.

PositioningStrapontin is a programme you can book from, not a ticket shop with articles. It sells certainty and taste: you always know the full price, the end time and the language before you commit.

02Name and logo
Strapontin Strapontin

A strapontin is the fold-down seat at the end of a row, in cinemas, theatres and the métro. It works for both cinema and theatre, it is unmistakably Parisian, and it carries the promise: there is a place for you, even at the last minute.

The mark is that seat seen from the side: an upright in Vintage Wine and a folded seat pad in Orangeade. It tilts when hovered, as if someone were about to sit. The wordmark is Bodoni Moda Bold with tight tracking, always capitalised as a proper noun, never all caps.

Use
  • Minimum height 20 px on screen.
  • Clear space equal to the width of the seat mark.
  • On Vintage Wine, the wordmark turns Cream and the seat pad stays Orangeade.
Avoid
  • The old full stop after the name: it reads as a magazine masthead.
  • Recolouring the seat pad. It is the only fixed use of Orangeade outside buttons.
  • Placing the logo on a poster pattern.

Why this splitWarm paper and wine-dark ink read as print and velvet seats, not as a tech product, which is what the art-house segment trusts. Reserving Orangeade for booking means the eye learns in seconds where to act; that directly supports conversion. Golden Green and Blue Heaven label facts the French audience uses to judge quality and suitability (labels, language version), so those facts are found by colour before they are read.

Rules
  • Never use Orangeade for decoration, links or tags.
  • Small text is always Vintage Wine or the muted ink #6A4A4E (6:1). Golden Green text uses #6B5204.
  • Cards sit on a lighter paper, #F7EFE4, with a 1.5 px wine outline. No grey, no pure white, no drop-shadow blur on controls.
Not in the palette
  • Red for urgency or scarcity. There are no countdowns and no "2 seats left".
  • Green for success. Confirmation is shown by the printed ticket itself.
  • A dark mode, for now. The paper is part of the brand; revisit once the identity is settled.

WhyThe audit found showtime boxes did not look clickable and showed no price. A ticket is the one object everyone knows you take.

The circled word

Que voir demain ?

One italic word per page, circled by hand in Orangeade, like a pen mark on a printed programme. On the homepage it is the active time filter and redraws when the filter changes.

WhyIt ties the brand gesture to the main task, so the most decorative element is also the clearest status indicator.

Geometry
  • Outlines 1.5 px Vintage Wine. Dashed lines mean "tear here" or "price detail".
  • Pills for controls, 10 px corners for tickets, 16 px for cards, 6 px for posters.
  • Hard offset shadow (3 px, 4 px, no blur) only on things you can press.
Layout
  • Hairline rules divide sections, as in a printed programme.
  • Tickets are the only element allowed to scroll sideways.
  • One filled colour block per screen at most.
06Design rules
  1. The first screen answers what, where, when and how much.The homepage hero is the finder. Supports the core task and the CNC finding that 75% choose by proximity.
  2. Full price, end time and language on every showtime, at every step.France scores 86 on uncertainty avoidance. No fee appears late; the refund and exchange policy is visible from the header to the payment page.
  3. No urgency, no crowd signals.No countdowns, stock warnings, rankings or "trending". Legitimacy comes from critics, laurels, labels and named programmers.
  4. Personal, not social.Bookmark on every poster, follow on every venue and season, a list that works without an account. Default quantity is one ticket. These are the engagement hooks.
  5. Nothing out of date is ever bookable.Dates are computed from today. Started screenings are struck through and cannot be opened in the booking flow.
  6. Show the network.Every film card states how many venues, the next showtime and the lowest full price. Special screenings come from several venues.
  7. French first, English one tap away.Titles and venue names stay French. Interface, synopsis and version codes are explained in English.
  8. The outing is a pleasure.Each venue names its bar or café, and the e-ticket suggests it. Restrained: one line, no upsell.
  9. Reward coming back.The carnet has its own tariff line in booking and its own section. Heritage seasons can be followed.
  10. Accessible by default.Skip link, visible focus, 4.5:1 text contrast, labels of 12.5 px or more, wheelchair filter, reduced-motion support, equal "Refuser" and "Accepter" cookie buttons.
07Motion and voice
Motion
  • Motion confirms an action; it never asks for attention.
  • Tickets lift and tear, posters tilt, the bookmark pops, the e-ticket prints downward.
  • 150–450 ms, one easing curve. Everything switches off under reduced-motion.
  • No auto-playing carousel. The second feature sits beside the first.
Voice
  • A well-read friend at the box office: precise, warm, brief.
  • Says "C'est réservé." not "Félicitations !".
  • States facts with numbers: "Échange gratuit jusqu'à 2h avant".
  • Inclusive French forms (un·e cinéaste). British English, 24-hour clock.

Still to validateThe programme, venues and prices are fictional. Payment is simulated; the real build hands card entry to Stripe's hosted form so no card data touches Strapontin. The identity has not yet been tested with users from the three segments, and the claim that people struggle to compare independent venues still needs research, as the project brief notes.
