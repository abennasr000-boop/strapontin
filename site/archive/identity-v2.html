Brand identity · version 2 · 14 November 2026 · working document

Strapontin, the seat there is always room for.

Version 1 was clear but loud: bold Didone type, geometric posters, hard outlines. The client review asked for the opposite temperature with the same clarity. Version 2 is quieter and warmer: film grain, hazy stills in instant-photo frames, a light literary serif and typewriter labels. Only what you can act on is drawn with a line.

01The feeling

The brand should feel like a programme found in a Left Bank café, with photos slipped inside: bohemian, light, a little dreamy, promising a discovery. It must never cost the reader certainty about price, time or language.

03Logo
Strapontin

A pen sketch of a fold-down seat seen from the side: the upright fixed to the wall, the seat pad tipped up on its hinge, the foot on the floor. The pad swings down when the logo is hovered. The wordmark is Cormorant Garamond italic, written as a name, never in capitals.

Why it changedThe v1 mark (a solid bar and a half-disc) did not read as a seat and resembled an existing platform's logo. A thin, slightly imperfect drawing matches the hand-made feeling and cannot be confused with a tech brand.
Status: first sketch. It needs a proper drawing pass and a legibility test at 20 px.

Faded print
  • Warm, washed cream; reads as old paper and daylight.
  • Closest to the beige grid-sheet pin.
  • Risk: can look sepia and flat if the stills are also warm.
Dusk
  • Cool grey-blue paper, Blue Heaven wall; reads as "l'heure bleue", the hour people go out.
  • More distinctive and dreamier.
  • Risk: cooler and less "paper"; orange accents must stay off the blue wall for contrast.

Fixed in both moodsOrangeade means "book" and nothing else. Raspberry and Golden Green are text colours, not fills. No red urgency, no green success state. Body text is Vintage Wine at 11:1 or more on paper.

06Images and shapes
The hazy still

Out-of-focus, grainy, vignetted, with a light leak. Heritage films are black and white. These are generated placeholders; real stills take the same treatment.

WhyA blurred still suggests a memory of a film instead of advertising it, which is the nostalgic register asked for. It also works when a venue has no artwork.

The instant photo

Off-white frame, typed caption, a degree or two of rotation, a soft shadow. Used for the gallery wall, the featured film and every venue portrait.

WhyThe polaroid is the centre of the mood board. It says "someone was there", which suits venues and discovery.

A fine ink line, square corners, paper fill. The ticket keeps its perforation and its stub still lifts on hover. No hard offset shadows, no pills.

WhyThe client kept the clarity of outlined actions but asked for the line to match the mood. Reserving the line for actions makes "what can I press" answerable at a glance.

Removed from v1
  • The circled headline word: it hurt readability and added nothing.
  • Geometric poster patterns and cropped giant initials.
  • Boxes around content, thick outlines, hard shadows, pill buttons.
Kept from v1
  • The illustrated venue façades, now inside polaroids with grain.
  • Ticket tear, bookmark pop, e-ticket printing.
  • All functional fixes from the audit.
07Design rules
  1. Atmosphere never hides a fact.Price, end time, language and venue are in plain type on every showtime. Grain and blur belong to images and paper, not to information.
  2. A line means you can act.Outlines are reserved for tickets, buttons, filters and fields.
  3. Every discovery ends on a showtime.Any image that invites exploring leads to a time, a place and a price within one interaction.
  4. No urgency, no crowd signals.Legitimacy comes from critics, laurels, labels and named programmers.
  5. Personal, not social.Bookmark, follow, a list that works without an account. Default quantity is one.
  6. Nothing out of date is bookable.Dates come from today; started screenings are struck through.
  7. French first, English one tap away.
  8. Accessible by default.Skip link, visible focus, 4.5:1 text, 12.5 px minimum labels, reduced-motion support, equal cookie buttons.
